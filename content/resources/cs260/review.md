## My Thoughts

My feelings for this module are a little split, since some teaching was good, some just okay. There were two organisers for the module this year: one covered algorithms, the other covered reductions and all the NP stuff.

### Teaching

The first half of teaching was okay: This portion covered complexity, greedy strategy, divide and conquer, graphs and DP, which in reality is not much content, but multiple (overcomplicated) examples are given. This felt rather drawn out and excessive.

The second half of teaching was much better, being more appropriately paced and engaging. This part covered problem definition, including NP, NP-Complete, NP-Hard and reductions, as well as flow networks (which I still don't understand icl). The examples used in this half were much easier to understand, mainly because they had little prerequisite knowledge.

### Seminars

Seminars were mostly engaging, although sometimes hard to follow as some of the solutions given weren't intuitive, although this may simply be because I did not go through the seminar sheets and content in enough time.

### Problem Sheets

The problem sheets this year were not too difficult to work through, and were not particularly notable, other than the sneakily hidden text to catch out people copy-pasting the questions into ChatGPT, which I'm very happy to see.

### Class Test

The first half of this test used a specific use case of greed algorithms (namely the Cashier's Algorithm) which was only briefly mentioned in lectures. I had not revised the material with this level of granularity, so this massively threw me off in the second section, which from memory, was focussed on reductions. Due to personal circumstances and being thrown off, I did poorly in this test.

### Exam

The exam structure was different this year, without much warning. Most notably, the first question was book work, which I find frustrating, and I think that this should not be the point of the module, especially since these examples were hard to understand, given their prerequisite knowledge which dipped into mathematics and even biology. The questions about reductions and NP were typically more enjoyable and easier to understand than those covering algorithm implementation.
