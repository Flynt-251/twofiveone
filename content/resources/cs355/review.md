> Please note this module review is based on teaching in the 2025/26 academic year. Remember that module contents, organisers, courseworks and exams are all subject to change.

## 1 - Why did I pick this module?

I'm a bit of a Privacy Nut. I wanna know *just* how much you can learn from a single picture, besides the metadata, of course.

## 2 - Workload

The workload for this module is paced out very well, it's a module that will demand *some* attention, but not enough to induce a ton of stress (unless you're really lacking in your linear algebra skills). Even when you're revising and doing past papers, they shouldn't present too much challenge.

## 3 - Assessments

You'll have a series of assessed labs you'll do, along with a final exam.

### Labs

The main struggle with this one is dealing with the joys of MATLAB, which in all fairness, is like Python with its own quirks and funny interface, it's not as bad as some make it out to be. The assessed labs have a clear end goal which means most struggles you'll run into will be because of your implementations. The marking is generally decent, however one of the labs in this cohort got seriously botched, to the point where some people gained 10% in marks.

### Exam

If you know what a matrix is, and can recall a couple of equations, you'll do just fine in this exam, the only other challenge is remembering the actual content itself and reiterating it on the answer sheet.

## 4 - Teaching Quality

The teaching for this module is okay, if anything, the motivation to do this module is what carried it for me, rather than the teaching itself. The teaching assistants were not particularly helpful however, at one point suggesting I go and speak to one of my colleagues! They additionally condoned using LLMs for support, however the module organiser explicitly advised against this, so take away from that what you will.

## 5 - Content and Difficulty

Everything is laid out in a way that makes sense and the content flows pretty naturally, you start by finding out how images are stored, how we can save space storing an image, before you look into how various aspects of the image can be used to find out additional information, or improve its quality. It's all quite straightforward too, aside from the expressions for Fourier Transformation or Discrete Cosine Transformation, but you fortunately don't need to remember those. Unless you want to, for some reason.

## 6 - Did I actually enjoy it?

Yes! It was genuinely cool to see how you could remove repeating noise using transformations, use maths to identify dodgy photoshopping or find out what camera took a picture by essentially exploiting manufacturing defects. That, and I realised while revising for the exam that it's surprisingly easy.

## 7 - Who's this module for?

If you're also a privacy, or even a cybersecurity nut, you'll definitely get a kick out of this module. Provided, the techniques used today tend to be more sophisticated, using machine learning to identify features, but even still, even using rather simple maths can yield some interesting results.

## 8 - The big tip

Take a bit of time to get used to MATLAB and its quirks with matrices. You can transpose a matrix with just one character and incidentally mess something up. (I would know)

## Final Verdict

4/10 difficulty, Further Maths who?

Interesting, cool, and easy. Pretty high score - 8/10.
