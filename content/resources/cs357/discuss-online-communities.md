## Who would you like to be today?

*You could be a CS student, a Greek God, or even a wolf that walks on two legs!*

**Profiles** are a major part of social media - they may or may not reflect your real self, and if not, they may represent a particular part of your identity and adhere to a particular network or platform. In some cases, these are completely anonymous, and you probably know exactly what platforms I'm on about.

Originally, we started from bulletin board systems (BBSs), before we grew to chatrooms like AOL, then to online forums, until we reach the likes of today, with platforms like Twitter (or X for you tech bros, yeuch), Reddit, Instagram, YouTube or TikTok, which facilitate billions of users talking about every topic under and away from the sun. This should immediately raise alarm bells and encourage you to act with caution, *anyone* can see your profile, posts and interactions, and many platforms use this information to direct ad revenue and perform marketing. What's more, anyone can use the information you share against you - posted a selfie in front of your house? Consider how easy it is to look on Google Street view and find your address.

Remember in our data discussion that you need some level of privacy in order to maintain your identity, autonomy and social relationships - what do you have if everything is out in the open? Well, marketability. In fact, you may already be aware that social media marketing has its own research field and is even a perfectly valid job in today's world - even your university performs social media market research!

This can be used for rather novel things, like a summary of your digital life - this was done back in 2015, with the "This is your digital life" app for Facebook by GSR, which got users to fill out a personality quiz, and share their Facebook data. You might also know GSR as Cambridge Analytica. Yes, THAT Cambridge Analytica, the same ones who [harvested Facebook data to assist Trump's 2016 political campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Facebook%E2%80%93Cambridge_Analytica_data_scandal), and quite possibly Brexit (ahh, good to see some things never change, huh?).

Social media gets you to divulge a lot of information, whether you like it or not. Much of it can be turned off, but it requires awareness and proactivity. It's important that you decide for yourself how far you're willing to go, and RTFPP - (Read the f\*\*\*ing privacy policy).

## Don't lose your head

It may feel quite natural to compare online communities with offline ones, but they are still significantly different - offline communities form due to common traits, like neighbours, religion, or nationality. By contrast, all you need to join an online community is an interest: trains, cars, toys, cartoons, anything. There's also much looser structure in online communities, no central authority or person to report to. Sure, this isn't entirely true, some places have dedicated moderators, but these are still volunteers, and usually don't have very much accountability, given many of them go by pseudonyms.

Particularly, many people worry of the risks of these communities.

- **They can be misused** - Malicious communities such as the reddit thread /r/iykyk (before you ask, child porn) can pop up.
- **They can facilitate stalking, harassment or cyberbullying** - If you're anonymised, and can be such anytime anywhere, this makes it easy to spew hate at anyone you dislike.
- **They put children at risk** - A child can easily sign up for YouTube or Facebook or, worst of all, Twitter. This makes them liable to being groomed, or worse.
	- A notable example here is [The Murder of Breck Bednar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Murder_of_Breck_Bednar), a 14-year-old boy who was groomed online and then murdered by Lewis Daynes.
- **There are known psychological effects** - Just look at the [Wikipedia Article for Internet Addiction Disorder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internet_addiction_disorder).

## For... everyone?

You may already know that free speech is often an issue tied to Social Media, as we balance allowing people to say what they want, and trying to eliminate misinformation and extremist rhetoric. It's also worth considering whether online activities should have consequences in the real world, like grooming a child, as we discussed earlier, though maybe not for [light-hearted shitposting about your job](https://www.businessinsider.com/michigan-man-says-posting-facebook-meme-led-to-lawsuit-bankruptcy-2023-5).

Another light to consider is pornography. Yes, seriously. And I'm not talking about mainstream platforms like "The Hub" (they don't need my publicity), I mean independent writers and artists. There is absolutely a risk that children are exposed to mature content, but this clashes with artists' right to expression. In fact, this is a conflict that is actively ongoing on platforms such as BlueSky, which very recently came under fire for [issuing account-wide adult content labels](https://github.com/bluesky-social/social-app/issues/10665), which effectively censor some accounts for users who disable adult content - is this justified or over-the-top?

## Billions must learn to feel accepted.

*# But I'm a chud, I'm an inceeeel... #*

Yep. Now it's time we talk about Incels. Originally, the term **Incel**, short for *Involuntary Celibate*, was coined by a single woman in Canada, who was expressing her struggle in trying to get into a romantic relationship, meant to be applicable to anyone. Pretty innocuous, right? Well, then the big, bad wolves that are 4Chan and Reddit stepped in, with mostly male groups using the term to describe their inability to get into relationships. But instead of using this to form support groups, they expressed their frustrations on wider society and pushing for the re-adoption of traditional gender roles, the **"Red Pill"** view, so to speak.

The rhetoric claims that women pick which men they date, and that 80% of women only want the top 20% of men... whatever the fuck that means. This leads to a very dark negative feedback loop with lots of incitement of violence and misogyny, which has even transpired into full on attacks on women and even [mass murders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Incel#Mass_murders_and_violence). Some members are even suicidal, or so defeated by the idea they will supposedly never get into a relationship, that they become nihilistic, often referred to as the *"Black Pill"*.
