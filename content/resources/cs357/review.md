> Please note this module review is based on teaching in the 2025/26 academic year. Remember that module contents, organisers, courseworks and exams are all subject to change.

## 1 - Why did I pick this module?

I took A-Level Sociology, so I've always had that essay itch while I've been at uni, and this felt like the best place to satisfy it.

## 2 - Workload

Be prepared to do a LOT of writing, and quite a bit of reading. And some of your own reasoning too. The challenge of this module comes from expressing your opinions on topics, but doing so while justifying your viewpoint and backing up with evidence. Don't worry too much though, writing doesn't take as long as you think once you get the ball rolling.

## 3 - Assessments

You'll need to do an essay, with a plan, and an exam.

### Essay

You'll be given a topic to explore, which you then plan and write an essay for. Depending on who you are, this will either be exciting or very daunting. I've experienced both sides. Don't be afraid to take this in your own direction by the way, this'll make sure you're writing passionately, which itself will make a great motivator. In my case, I sadly had to change quite a bit of the essay last-minute, but even then I found the marking to be quite agreeable.

### Exam

Again, be prepared to write. A lot. You'll be able to put together a crib sheet, so don't worry too much about remembering a boatload of case studies and perspectives, instead focus more on re-mastering things like PEA, PEEL and so on. The first half is mainly making sure you know the content, before you're hit with a MASSIVE 60 mark essay question. Remember those days?

## 4 - Teaching Quality

I thoroughly enjoyed going to the lectures and seminars in this module, mainly because these opened up conversations you don't really see anywhere else in computer science. The lecturer was also very good at being engaging yet informative, meaning I didn't have to strain to keep up.

## 5 - Content and Difficulty

Technically speaking, the term "content" is loose here. Sure, you'll want to remember various ethical perspectives, but after that? Bring your own case studies and evidence! The open-ended-ness of this module means it's kind of up to you what the content is. And in a similar vein, the concept of "difficulty" is ambiguous in the CS context. Do you like writing? Do you like having what some may consider to be difficult conversations? Then this module may be easier for you than it is to someone who doesn't like these things.

## 6 - Did I actually enjoy it?

Thoroughly. Like I said, it's not often you get to have fulfilling ethical discussions about Computer Science within the field, and this is a great opportunity for you to do so.

## 7 - Who's this module for?

All the people who love yapping, sharing ideas, debating, and of course, complaining about tech. Also helps if you like writing and/or did one or more humanities subjects at A-Level. Or at least kept your writing sharp.

## 8 - The big tip

Don't just regurgitate the module! Be willing to give your own perspective, to share it vocally or in writing. It'll make the lectures and seminars more rich, and more importantly, it'll help maintain your own passion when doing this module. I mean, I wrote about furries in my exam, that's how passionate I got about it!

## Final Verdict

Difficulty is in the eyes of the beholder, so 5/10.

Loved it, more people should take it and face the difficult conversations in Computer Science. 9/10.
