## The Rise of the Robots

*Once a shitty video game, now a possible threat?*

A robot could refer to one of many different things. We might immediately turn to the science fiction examples, like the Terminator or Bender from Futurama, as well as very real advancements in robotics like Boston Dynamics's Spot the Dog, or Paro, a baby seal plush designed to give companionship to the elderly. But wait, are our ideas of robots creating unconscious biases? This does tie back to algorithmic bias, but even that doesn't explain things like giving a cleaning robot a feminine appearance, or a robot with possibly racist biases.

The reason robots are interesting on their own and distinct from algorithms or AI, is because they ask us to consider moral responsibilities, not just on robots themselves, but also their design, and how humans treat them. Sure, we can (mostly) agree that robots shouldn't kill or harm humans, especially when we consider Asimov's (fictional) three laws of robotics, but what about the other way around? Is it acceptable to turn off a robot if it doesn't consent? [Is it acceptable to hurt, break or destroy a robot?](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HitchBOT) I think a lot of this discussion depends on how far we are willing to anthropomorphise robots and "implement sentience" (I put that in quotes because I think it's not possible, and even if it were, we wouldn't be stupid enough to mass produce sentient robots). These can sound like absurd questions, but in some ways, that's the point.

Let's get away from the theoretical for now and look at reality. It's no secret that there are a number of robots which are helping us to [automate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Outline_of_automation) certain tasks, from mowing gardens to replacing humans in mass production environments. Hmm, that last one is especially interesting, and represents how we've shifted away from manual labour work. Sure, we might initally see this as an inherently good thing, but remember, *not everyone has a degree*. Not everyone has A-Levels, or even GCSEs! So the first to lose their jobs are these "unskilled" workers (well, lower education more specifically). Is that acceptable, and have we considered the impact on such workers' lives if they are made redundant? It's not exactly like we can still keep them around to monitor robots' work, that requires an entirely different skill set!

Now back to theorising. Would you trust a robot to be an emotional companion? Some people would, there are tags on certain platforms for that stuff. But would your experience with that robot be authentic? What if we framed this as a gigantic machine which could emulate any scenario in your brain? Could this mislead people? I'm inclined to say no, none of this is authentic - the brain is easy to trick, but the body is not, I like to say. And as for being misleading, we'll talk about very real problems surrounding this later on. We might also talk about robots' appearance when talking about them as a companion - if we make a robot look too much like a human, this can trigger an uncanny valley response. It's entirely its own ethical debate whether humanoid robots should be a thing: I like to know if I'm talking to a real person myself, but that's just me.

## "So I put this through \[Insert LLM Here\]..."

*Pray I don't toss that laptop of yours out the window*

So, without further ado, let's talk about contemporary AI. This includes things like SlapGPT, Cloode, Deepshit or Chudpilot. I'm not using their real names as I don't want to give them publicity, you know what I'm talking about anyway, and my tone should immediately reveal what I think of them. Do LLMs and generative AI tools have genuine and innocuous use cases? Absolutely, but given the ways these tools are being used and abused, it's hard for me to not have at least a little contempt: [they're actively hurting artists and stealing their work](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3600211.3604681), ruining the fun in software engineering and [causing psychotic episodes](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12863933/). But, because AI is the latest in a long line of buzzwords for trigger-happy businessmen, it's everywhere - they're even shoving it in butt plugs (no seriously, that's not a joke).

The important thing to remember when discussing LLMs, diffusion models, or any sort of AI models is... they're exactly that. They're statistical models, and they don't "reason" the way some people believe they do. LLMs in particular are simply a fancy autocorrect, given your response, and billions of lines of language in context, they simply predict the next words to create a response that *looks* correct - it's not General AI, [it's just good at pandering to people](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.aec8352). This explains why CrapGPT can't do maths correctly, or why Gleebemi claims that the word "piss" is in "Wednesday", or why AI models tend to produce fake or non-existent sources.

Also, consider that when we use an LLM online, our inputs are usually being consumed as training data. *That's unacceptable in an enterprise setting*, what if trade secrets or customer data gets leaked this way? I mean, surely an AI model would never [allow access to someone's personal data or accounts](https://www.404media.co/hackers-simply-asked-meta-ai-to-give-them-access-to-high-profile-instagram-accounts-it-worked/) ([Archived 🔓](https://archive.is/7DLp2)), right? Well, never mind. And it's not like we can see HOW models come to the decisions, as dissecting them is very difficult. CS331 should have taught you that doing such requires a LOT of linear algebra and calculus, something we can't really translate into a user-friendly format. [Fortunately, we're working on it.](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11747-019-00710-5)

However, the good side of something like an LLM is they can provide a good starting point for making rough drafts of ideas or texts, which can help us get the ball rolling on a project. They're also great for summarising long texts, being able to pick out key points. And to be honest, though sometimes wonky, search assistants can provide good starting points when doing quick research, like finding the right terminal command for a Linux system, for instance. Just because I generally disapprove of AI, that doesn't mean I don't see its merits - I used to use KrabsGPT in Sixth Form to revise for my sociology exam!

## Generating Horrors beyond Eldritch

*Aw, sweet!*

Generate AI tools, namely Stable Diffusion models, allow us to generate images, videos and music, simply from a text prompt. At first, this sounds amazing, but then you realise what humans are capable of. This has huge implications if someone tries to pass AI generated content as real, and this has already happened, on multiple occasions, which is why we're seeing things like [BBC Verify](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BBC_Verify). This also opens the door to some VERY disturbing content, including, if left unchecked, [an endless stream of CSAM](https://arstechnica.com/tech-policy/2026/01/x-blames-users-for-grok-generated-csam-no-fixes-announced/). We also see other problems like plagarism, vectors for bullying and harassment, [sexualisation of people without their consent](https://www.technologyreview.com/2022/12/12/1064751/the-viral-ai-avatar-app-lensa-undressed-me-without-my-consent/), and identity theft.

This isn't even getting into the frankly ridiculous energy demand that comes with AI training, with [GPT3 generating roughly 552 tons of CO2 in its training](https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.10350). And that's not even getting into the consumption driven by all the thousands of people prompting it! All of that to produce some ugly, uncanny pictures of anthropomorphised logs, sharks wearing trainers, and... crab Jesus? Yes, seriously, those are all over Facebook, and there are plenty of AI bots in the comments spamming "Amen". Christ, [the internet really is dead](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dead_Internet_theory), isn't it?

## The solution to AI?

AI is not going away, that's for sure, it is definitely here to stay. But for now, all we can do is simply wait for that bubble to pop, and enjoy the show as the economics blows up in the investors' faces. In the meantime, we can still [do anything without the use of AI](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=R-edYoZZosg), just remember it takes some willingness to fail and get back up. That's not to say, don't use AI or you're an idiot, rather, **know its place as a tool, and not a replacement for work**. Tools are only effective when you're in control - not when you're the one taking the backseat.

It's up to you whether or not you consider AI art real art, and whether the automation of various jobs brought about by AI is a good or a bad thing, just like with robots. If anything though, it's important to work together to maintain ethical use of AI, and prevent harm to people using it. This is something that will take time, but as long as we talk to people outside the bubble of Computer Science, we will be getting somewhere.
