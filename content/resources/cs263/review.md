As someone who cares a lot about their privacy and computer security, I was quite interested in taking this module, and I can quite easily recommend this module to anyone else who's very interested in the subject. I'd be a little more careful if you only have a passing interest, though, and you'll see why shortly.

I've already made my own security suggestions here, so I won't repeat myself by linking to resources here.

## Teaching

Teaching for this module was okay, mostly consisting of reading off of lecture slides, although a few short demos were performed. I also appreciate the organiser's effort to record lectures themselves, for when lecture capture failed. That being said, I have had issues with the content of the module, and of the organiser's understanding.

The AI portion of the module feels largely unrelated to cybersecurity, although some components of this are relevant. That being said, the question of whether or not AI girlfriends and boyfriends are a detriment to society is certainly not relevant. In this same vein, the organiser has also cited ChatGPT as a source in their slides. I consider this to be unacceptable behaviour and I have openly condemned it.

If you've read the notes, you'll know that user agents on browsers are easy to spoof, and that some browsers even facilitate this for compatibility reasons. At one point in a lecture, I asked the organiser about this, to which they responded "possibly". I have since had reason to doubt the organiser's understanding. Nevertheless, they have shown clear passion for this subject, and for that I cannot fault them.

## Labs

The labs were a lot of fun to complete, not least because most of them were demonstrations of different types of attack, such as performing a reverse shell or hacking a vulnerable FTP client. They're not at all necessary, but I would recommend completing them regardless.

## Coursework

This year, the coursework had multiple components, namely writing a report about hacking tools, performing OSINT and then carrying out a theoretical cyber attack by using SQLi and developing ransomware. All in all, this was a lot of fun to carry out, and I even recall staying up at 3am writing my own ransomware which I dubbed "CANIS" (don't ask, it was 3am after all). Overcoming hurdles in problem solving felt very rewarding.

## Exam

The exam this year was a little easier than previous years, *but keep in mind*, the course content is subject to change each year, so you should rely more on the sample paper provided for your year more than previous papers.
