You know, after doing databases in A-Level CS, I really thought there couldn't be THAT much more to relational databases. That was before I learned that joins, grouping and relational calculus exist.

## Useful Resources

### W3Schools

Does this site need any introduction anymore? They have an [SQL tutorial](https://www.w3schools.com/sql/). Note that the database software you use (likely Postgres) will likely have some additional quirks you'll need to keep in mind.

Outside of this, there's not really anything else that's not linked in the module webpages which is useful, except maybe a litany of StackOverflow articles.

## My Thoughts

This is not a particularly interesting module, unless you love using SQL (If that's you, remember Wellbeing Support Services are a thing), so really, the best course of action is to work your way through the coursework, then use that knowledge you've gained about joins and aggregation in the final exam.

### Teaching

Teaching was about average for this module, and if you've taken A-Level CS, you can comfortably skip the first 5 lectures or so. After this point, subqueries, joins and views are introduced, which are not in the A-Level syllabus, so it's worth coming along to see some examples and pitfalls when using them. One of the organisers likes mentioning beer a lot in their slides, which leaves a bitter taste in my mouth (I'm more of a cider guy, although honestly the thought of alcohol in any capacity is making me feel ill rn).

### Labs

The labs are mostly there to get you familiar with the interfaces you'll use in the coursework, so they're not that hard, just a bit daunting since you'll be writing out some very long queries and scrubbing through lots of data.

### Coursework

At first, the coursework seemed quite daunting, given the number of different criteria that needed to be filled, but by writing these in a to-do list and focussing on each one individually, it was possible to get through all of it. Some of the criteria you can meet purely by defining your tables correctly! It also made a nice change writing a prelude/report in markdown, instead of writing a full report in LaTeX or making lots and lots of comments.

### Exam

The exam was of similar difficulty to last year, although Question 2 presented quite the gamble in terms of marks: 13 marks for a yes or no response, with a short justification. I'm not saying that's a good thing, to be clear.
