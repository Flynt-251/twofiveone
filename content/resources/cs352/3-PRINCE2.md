The **PRojects IN Controlled Environments 2 (PRINCE2)** methodology is a way of approaching projects, much like PMBOK is a framework for projects. What makes it distinct from PMBOK is its focus on deliverables and the business case. Its structure, while drawing similarities with PMBOK, is also very different.

- **Starting up a Project** - Creating the Project Mandate and initial specification
- **Directing a Project** - An omnipresent state which occurs in parallel to all the following stages, where we note our progress and gather feedback, then decide the next steps.
- **Initiating a Project** - Beginning the planning for the project and building on the specification.
- **Managing a Stage Boundary** - Planning a specific iteration of the project.
- **Managing Project Delivery** - The execution of the stage.
- **Controlling a Stage** - The monitoring and controlling of the execution of the project, which works parallel to project delivery.
- **Closing a Project** - The usual tying of loose ends, delivery to the client, and gathering of final feedback to feed into future projects.

Additionally, PRINCE2 is underpinned by a set of seven principles which help us properly direct a project (i.e. avoid going off on the wrong sort of tangent or become uncontrollable).

- **Continuous Business Justification** - Is the project feasible, viable, and capable of business benefit?
- **Learn from experience** - You can't hit the same wall the same way and expect a different result. If something didn't work out last time, change your approach!
- **Defined roles and responsibilities** - Who's responsible for what aspects of the project, and who's accountable for what?
- **Manage by Stages** - Split the project into more manageable iterations or stages. We covered how to do this already with WBS.
- **Manage by Exception** - You can expect your team to be competent, so let them get on with what you asked them to do, establishing limits and letting people work within them. Only get involved as a manager, when those limits are challenged.
- **Focus on Products** - What's the output of this? Don't focus on how or why.
- **Tailor to suit your project environment** - Ignore all of the above if it's the best! As much as PRINCE2 is a methodology, there is no set requirement for you to follow ALL the advice laid out here. Some things work better for smaller projects, others for bigger projects!

## You're fired

*Who the hell hired these idiots anyway?*

The project team consists of both a **Project Manager**, who handles day-to-day management, but also requires authorisation from the **Project Board**, who represents the project's stakeholders, to take significant steps and change plans. The project board may consist of higher-ups in the business, one or more of the end users, and a representative of the workers on the project - these people are responsible and held accountable for the project's overall success. The manager's main priority is supporting their team, they cannot decide anything without the permission of the project board.

## Papers, please

*Hello, little potato man!*

Remember that the project delivers *products*, not activities or "goals". We write a specification, make the project, and then assess its quality. This is what we call **project-based planning**, where at every stage of the product, we create something that we can show for it, to demonstrate that it happened, and have something whose quality we can assess.

What does that translate to? Papers! Well, it could also be database records, or video journal recordings or... whatever you define a "product" to be. You could do the whole thing in Minecraft or something. Here's the main idea.

- **Records** - A set of regular logs setting out what was learned, issues that came up, risks, configuration information, and so on.
- **Baselines** - Project Brief, Initiation, Business case, management strategy, stage plans, work packages... basically anything that sets out what we're doing, or at a low level, what has been done already.
- **Reports** - End of Stage, End of Project, Reflections, Lessons Learnt, Problems, and so on. The more high level of what we've done.

Remember that we only need as much as is useful, so if you start complaining and saying "PRINCE2 requires too much paperwork", well then you only have yourself to blame.

**Remember that PRINCE2 is not the end-all, be all for projects.** It's simply a scalable, iterative methodology where you only manage by exception. As much as it's a great help, you've still got to put work in as a manager, listen to your workers and the project board, and, most importantly, not get full of yourself.
