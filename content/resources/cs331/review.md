> Please note this module review is based on teaching in the 2025/26 academic year. Remember that module contents, organisers, courseworks and exams are all subject to change.

## 1 - Why did I pick this module?

Honestly, I picked this because I didn't have many other options. I really don't care much for AI or machine learning, so I figured, based on the fact many people in 4th year told me this was an easy module, that I may as well give this a shot.

## 2 - Workload

This was, on most accounts, a back burner module. The problem sheet is fairly short and can be over and done with in a matter of days. Add on top of that, the fact much of the exam consists of testing your reasoning and ability to do A-Level calculus (if that), this module won't add much to your workload. You *could* attend the labs if they're interesting to you, but I really wasn't in the mood to mess around with Jupyter notebooks and deal with libraries I had no experience in.

## 3 - Assessments

There are two components, a problem sheet, and an exam. The split is 20/80.

### Problem Sheet

Most of the problem sheet could be done by an A-Level student, however there are a couple of problems which had me sketching out solutions over and over again, trying to observe hidden patterns that didn't actually exist, until something kinda clicked. The problem sheet this year was *significantly* harder than previous years - many of us suspect this was done out of necessity, as the module organiser liked to brag about how 70% of people pass the module without a hitch. Genuinely, the two last problems of the last two questions of this problem sheet are probably someone's thesis subject.

The feedback was abysmal. Many of us got the same, very generic feedback, which might have been copy-pasted from \[insert commercially available LLM here\], enough to make me feel as though nobody actually looked at my submission. Oh, it also came out late.

### Exam

The exam would have been unremarkable, were it not for the fact there were questions that contain content outside the module, or more accurately, were beyond the scope of the resources available, namely Grid LSTMs and some extensions to generative models. Beyond that, if you can do differentiation and add things together on a calculator, you'll be fine.

## 4 - Teaching Quality

Expect a lot of reading off of slides, presentations with, way, WAY too many animations, and to zone out very easily if you're not buzzing to learn about neural networks. I most often than not, drifted out of lectures mentally if I wasn't skipping them in favour of a seminar for an interdisciplinary module I was taking at the same time. It's nice for the organiser to include pre-recorded videos for those who want to catch up outside of lectures, however these are few and far between, but more crucially, **replace any sort of lecture capture**. This puts you at a disadvantage for any new content, as it did for me in a couple of cases. I wasn't around for the lecture when Grid LSTMs were covered, for instance.

## 5 - Content and Difficulty

About the hardest thing for me in this module was wrapping my head around complex structures like the composition of an LSTM unit and the mathematical expression for a General Adversarial Network. Outside of that, the things you learn are rather basic, but constitute the building blocks for then understanding what a neural network is, and how to work with it.

## 6 - Did I actually enjoy it?

No, because I'm quite indifferent to AI (or, given how it was forced down my throat this year, an "anti"). It's not a particularly great introduction to the whole subject. This module did, however, help form my opinion on AI, in that it's really just sophisticated brute forcing with a PC. It's literally spamming calculus at a problem and seeing what happens. This is probably a decent module if you like AI, unlike myself.

## 7 - Who's this module for?

Anyone who's also planning on doing, or has already done modules about machine learning or AI, as I anticipate you will have far more overlap which will work to your advantage.

## 8 - The big tip

You shouldn't necessarily take a module because it is "easy". That word will mean different things for everyone, and motivation will take you further than any promise of a reduced workload.

## Final Verdict

6/10 difficulty, some thinking required.

I really was not a fan of this module, mainly because the teaching is pretty lacklustre, and, to be blunt, this module is not up to shape at all. 4/10.
