Uh oh, group work.

I won't link any resources here, as there's not really anything of use I can provide. That being said, there is useful information in the notes (that's what they're there for!).

## Teaching

The teaching for this module is great, with content delivered in a very engaging style. I would recommend coming to lectures, as they will explain a litany of concepts you need to know for the exam, although it is possible to extract this information from the slides. Then again, you'll miss out on the questions at the start, which in this case, included a rant about Death Note. That's not a joke, that really did happen: the lecturer even proceeded to take a bite of a chocolate bar like Mello. Go read or watch Death Note, by the way.

## Hell.

*By which I mean the group project*

That being said, my group was overall very responsive and everyone seemed to pull their weight: I got lucky, and some other people did not, I have heard horror stories unfortunately. We decided to split work based on our own strengths, i.e. based on what degree we were doing (one of us was a Discrete Maths student), what languages we knew, and what our past experience was. Initially, the plan and starting reports we submitted were marked poorly, which we attribute to poor definitions of what were being asked, although others have noted that this is simply a simulation of industry, and not poor specification. However, our final project turned out very well, even winning the competition that was held with Dorset Software, this year's sponsor for the module. I'll talk more about the project in the projects section of this website.

## Exam

The exam was of similar difficulty and structure to previous years: no particular notes here. I will say it did make me glad I bought a smartwatch this year (if you know, you know).
