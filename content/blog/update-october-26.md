I've made multiple drafts of this post, because I'm very much aware of the fact I haven't posted in over a year. Predictably, this is because my work got the best of me during the academic year and so I didn't really have the energy to post. But it's also a little more than that - several things have happened in and outside of my degree which have caused some shake-ups, so let's discuss all of that.

## A shift in future endeavours

This site, "twofiveone.xyz", started out as any other portfolio website. Y'know, make over-the-top claims as to my skills, show off impressive sounding software projects I'd worked on, so on. But I decided that Software Engineering really isn't for me - my pass in algorithms last year was borderline and working on my dissertation felt like a chore: ironic since it was something I originally had very high hopes for.

So what *do* I want to do then? Well, I don't think there's much sense in locking myself into one specific career path this early on. Rather, I know what sort of things I'd prefer to do, and that's where I'd like to go from. I figure this way, it keeps my horizons broad and helps keep me from feeling jaded in the job search. What I am passionate about is working on server hardware, getting things up and running, and helping other people understand computers. This more fits in line with Systems Reliability Engineering - if that's a career path that sounds more fitting to your tastes as well, I'd suggest doing some research into this.

So what does this have to do with twofiveone? Well, its purpose will now shift away from frontloading for businesses - there's no super-fancy animations done in javascript that functionally act as overcompensation. I want what's on here to be genuine. So the focus will now **mainly shift to making useful, informative content.** That will be through me continuing to produce notes for my modules next year, and writing blogs about my experiences with technology.

## We need to let complex things be complex

[Reject Convenience](https://youtube.com/@RejectConvenience) has been one of the channels that has maintained my hope in technology. Their videos discuss various aspects of technology which impact our everyday privacy, all in a way that's approachable to a non-technical audience, AND doing so with incredible levels of production quality. Forgive me projecting here, but I very much resonate with their goal in helping people understand technology - for me, this is how we can deal with a lot of the issues we are facing in technology.

You may or may not be aware of the fact that [the UK government is providing "AI Training"](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/cp37prvp072o), which is... [a generous term for it](https://bsky.app/profile/kolektiv.xyncro.com/post/3mdiafhcphk2t). What really bugs me is how AI used to refer to some really cool stuff, but now has become a buzzword for Large Language Models, an innovation that I feel has gained more hype than it deserves. I feel like most people would not be able to understand that AI refers to many different systems - like, the AI they use for protein folding in medical labs is not the same AI as what goes into ChatGPT. The goal then, should be to help the public understand how AI works, which should then help them how to use it... right? Unfortunately, everyone seems to only be interested in *using* the technology, not how it works. Which I can only imagine at least partially leads to [people getting hurt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deaths_linked_to_chatbots).

You might think it's impossible to explain to the public how AI works. But I'm certain it's possible, and the existence of creators like Reject Convenience is proof. And it's not just AI, but also computers in general, cloud storage, self-hosting, and so on. **If we want the technological future we dream of, we need to properly educate the public so that they can express their digital autonomy.**

## And on AI...

This year, I took an interdisciplinary class called "Creating Digital Futures" - I figured this would give me a holistic view on technology. What I left with, was a great disdain for AI. Not hard to imagine when you consider that on the second seminar I attended, we had a guest lecturer tell me, *"You don't even need a marketing degree, you can just have AI do it all for you".* Let me tell you, that was the closest I have ever been to vomiting in my adulthood, without getting drunk.

For context, you might know that I'm very supportive of indie artists and have even contributed my own money to commissioning then (yes, it's exactly what it sounds like). Generative AI has become a genuine threat to these artists, **including writers, like myself.** Many of us are now expected to compete with a never-ending stream of slop which is being held up by billion-dollar corporations who will happily take money from people who don't have the patience to build up genuine creative skills. You can go online and see a litany of tutorials on how to use AI to create endless slop and profit - there's no pride or joy in that!

What's more, I believe this will cause the demise of the software industry - too many times I have heard of people doing nothing more than puppet agents to develop software, only for nobody to understand how it works, and for user experience to decline. I have no desire to contribute to such a creatively repressed environment.

The bottom line is, **I don't want to use AI in my work**. At best, my own interactions with chatbots have been negligibly helpful, and I don't source them for anything, not even inspiration for this blog. **The contents of my blogs and notes on this website are made by humans, for humans.**

## Where's the "Projects" section?

**I got rid of it.** Software is not my primary focus any more, and if I feel the need to talk about a project I've worked on, I'll do that on a CV, not here. I'm not even particularly proud of everything I've put together in certain circumstances. Really, I've not had any motivation to update it, because I don't always enjoy working on software that much. Sure, I might have the occasional project I put together, or the coursework I'm rather proud of, but I'd rather let CVs, Git commits and me do the talking on that.

## Welcome (back) to the REAL Internet

It should be no surprise that as a child, I spent quite an appreciable amount of time sat at a computer. From my dad's old Sony Vaio, to a hand-me-down Surface laptop, all the way to my ThinkPad, the first laptop I bought with my own money. Sure, I spent some time on an iPad, but most of my online life has been spent on a non-touch screen interface. As such, I've learned a huge amount of information by simply surfing the net.

My journey in computing got a huge kick start with channels like [The 8-Bit Guy](https://www.youtube.com/@The8BitGuy), who got me interested in learning how computers work. From there, I then spent loads of time learning things I could do in Python, like making Discord Bots and Web Scraping the prices of Pokémon cards (back before both scraping and Pokémon cards were ruined), and much much more. The point I'm trying to make here is, **the availability of free information is a major reason I am where I am today.** Now that I have reached a stage where I have learned almost everything I'd want to know, I now feel it's my duty to do what others did for me, share my knowledge.

Another thing. If you've been around for long enough, you may feel as though the internet has changed from what it once was - what was once a place of self-expression and whimsy, has seemingly been replaced with cold-hearted, corporate social media websites. But... that "old" internet never really died. It's now just called [The Web Revival](https://thoughts.melonking.net/guides/introduction-to-the-web-revival-1-what-is-the-web-revival). **This website, twofiveone.xyz, is part of this revival, being an indie website intended on being Net Positive.** What that means is, I use this site as an opposition to corporate junk, and as a way for me to express myself creatively - I like writing stuff, and if that writing can help other people, then that's a great motivator for me to keep writing!

I don't see the point in locking myself into an existing platform like Twitter, Substack or even Wordpress - by having everything on my own website, I remain in control. This site cannot be bought out, censored, subjected to [Algospeak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algospeak), or some other crap that would upset shareholders or lobbyists. So, here's what that means in short.

- Everything on this website is free for you to read and share, forever.
- The code for this website is [open-source](https://github.com/Flynt-251/twofiveone). Feel free to take inspiration and make your own website! (I'm especially proud of the navigation box I made :D)
- No trackers. No cookies, tracking pixels, fingerprints, none of that bullshit. I'm not interested in harvesting your data.
- No ads or other forms of monetisation. Advertising detracts from the experience, supports some of the worst people in the world, and is just annoying. I don't want your money anyway.
- We're in this together - Want to suggest a correction or improvement? Reach out! Made your own cool indie website? Get in touch and let's link together!

## This is a means of expression

Over the past year, I've done a ton of crazy things - skip school to fly to Sweden, attend a convention during my exams, raise money for charity in a weekend-long livestream, and way, way more. It's all taught me a lot about my own identity and how I choose to perceive myself. I want twofiveone to help me in this journey too. So, time and schedule permitting, I will aim to provide more frequent blog posts, ideally one per month.

Failing that, I also have an idea to make a Substack account where I post "side quests", basically anything I don't feel fits into my usual blog posts about tech, things like gaming, cooking or heck, it could just be me talking about wolves.

Again, I want to shift away from this past version of the website where it was just a heartless portfolio. Anyone can market themselves - we all do it in the pursuit of work, whether we actually want to or not. But I think a strong character says more than a pile of projects and generic blog posts on a fancified website, especially in a world where it's easy to get lost in a sea of algorithms. The need to stand out has become more important than ever.

And what's more, it's becoming more and more apparent to me that the tech industry is one of the least ethical industries in the world. Which is hard to stomach when you want to do the right thing. So let this website be an outlet for me to at least try.
